# Regulation basics

Regulation is the set of laws and rules applied to cryptocurrency activity. This domain gives a **neutral, non-legal** technical overview: how regulators classify assets, what KYC/AML obligations exist, and why jurisdiction matters — it is an explanation, not legal advice.

## Core concepts

- **Classification** — whether an asset is treated as a commodity, security, property, currency, or "virtual asset" varies by jurisdiction and changes obligations around issuance, trading, and taxes.
- **KYC (Know Your Customer)** — regulated services must verify customer identity before providing services.
- **AML (Anti-Money Laundering)** — obligations to monitor, report, and prevent money laundering and terrorist financing; formalized by the FATF Recommendations and the Travel Rule.
- **Sanctions** — compliance with international sanctions affects who may be served and where.

## Common themes

- Exchanges, custodians, and payment processors face the heaviest licensing and reporting duties.
- Self-custody and open-source software are generally treated differently from financial services.
- Disclosure and investor-protection laws increasingly apply to token sales and offerings.

## Why it matters technically

- Node operators and open-source maintainers usually do not act as financial intermediaries, but **anyone operating a business with crypto must analyze local rules**.
- The regulatory treatment of an asset determines its tax regime and transfer restrictions — relevant to on-chain engineers building products.
