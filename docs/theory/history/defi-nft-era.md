# DeFi and NFT eras

The 2020s brought a wave of applications on top of programmable blockchains: **decentralized finance (DeFi)** — financial primitives without intermediaries — and **non-fungible tokens (NFTs)** — unique digital items on-chain. Both rest on smart-contract infrastructure built during the Ethereum era.

## DeFi

DeFi uses smart contracts to recreate financial services without a bank or broker:

- **DEX/AMM** — Uniswap and Curve allow trustless token swaps via liquidity pools.
- **Lending** — Aave and Compound let users supply/borrow assets with algorithmic interest.
- **Stablecoins** — DAI (collateralized) and USDT/USDC (pegged) move value with reduced volatility.
- **Aggregators** — routing across venues for best price (1inch, Paraswap).

See the [DeFi domain]({{ 'defi' | relative_url }}) for the tool catalog.

## NFTs

NFTs are unique tokens (typically ERC-721/EIP-1155) representing ownership of digital items: art, collectibles, game assets, and membership.

- **Blast off** — CryptoKitties (2017) showed demand; the 2021 boom made NFTs mainstream.
- **Marketplaces** — OpenSea, Blur, and Rarible trade ERC-721 tokens.
- **Beyond JPEGs** — provenance, royalties, and utility tokens; many tied to Web3 games and DAOs.

## What to know technically

- Both build on smart-contract standards (ERC-20/721/1155).
- DeFi security is a real concern: hacks and reentrancy bugs have cost billions — see [Smart-contract development]({{ 'dev-tools' | relative_url }}).
- "DeFi summer" (2020) marked the rapid expansion of liquidity mining and yield protocols.
